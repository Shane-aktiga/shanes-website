04 · Drive

A little competitive streak

I have always enjoyed racing, but it was not until I found sim racing that I really got hooked.

My introduction came through The Track at Asheville, a local sim racing centre where I met Joel Bergstein and a great group of people who all shared a love of racing. Joel deserves a special shoutout for getting me into the sport. What started as a fun way to spend some time behind the wheel turned into a hobby that has stuck with me ever since.

There is no shortage of fancy equipment in sim racing, but the best part has always been the racing itself. The competition, the challenge of getting better, and the experience of racing wheel to wheel with other people are what keep me coming back.

Thursday night racing

These days I do most of my racing online. I also host regular Thursday night league races for a group of folks who were regulars at The Track. It has been a great way to keep that community together, enjoy some friendly competition, and occasionally remind ourselves that we are not nearly as fast as we think we are.

For me that is really what sim racing is about: the people, the competition, and the fun of racing. Racing online was an adjustment after racing in a room with everyone. You lose the part where somebody leans over the back of your seat and tells you what you did wrong. You keep the racing.

Building something for the sim racing community

My interest in sim racing has also turned into a chance to work on technology for the sport. I have been supporting Joel as he develops SimOps, an operations platform built to help sim racing centres run their businesses.

We are also exploring a couple of related products that will be available independently to the broader sim racing community. It is still early and there is more to come, but it has been fun finding ways to combine racing with the software work.

And who knows, maybe some of this will help me get a little faster. Probably not. But it is worth a shot.
