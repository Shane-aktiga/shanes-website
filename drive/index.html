04 · Drive

I race on Thursday nights with people I met in a brick building

Sim racing, which I got into sideways and stuck with because of the people. The building is gone now. The Thursday races are not.

I got into sim racing at The Track at Asheville, a sim racing centre in a brick building downtown. My friend Joel Bergstein introduced me to it. He is the reason I race at all, and I would not have found my way in on my own.

What hooked me was the racing, not the gear. I know people who love building rigs, and I enjoy talking to them, but I have never wanted to be the person with the spreadsheet of pedal options. I want a grid, a start, and twenty minutes of trying to be slightly less sloppy than the person ahead of me.

Thursday nights

The Track has closed, but the group did not really want to stop racing together. So I host a league on Thursdays for the people who used to show up there. Same crowd, same arguing about corner entry, different building. Mine, mostly, which has fewer snacks.

Racing online was an adjustment after racing in a room with everyone. You lose the part where somebody leans over the back of your seat and tells you what you did wrong. You keep the racing.

SimOps

Joel is building SimOps, an operations platform for sim racing centres, and I am helping him with it. Running a centre turns out to involve a lot of software that nobody has written properly yet.

We are also looking at a few standalone tools for the wider sim racing community. Nothing I want to describe in detail before it exists.

Link needed. There is no SimOps URL on this page yet because I do not have one. Send it over and it takes one line.
