05 · Build

Twenty years with a tool belt

Framing, finish work, the whole range. It is the longest thing I have ever done and people tend to skip past it on the way to the software.

I spent more than twenty years as a carpenter. It was not a stop on the way to something else. It was the job, for a long time, and I was good at it.

Nothing is ever square

The first thing the trade teaches you is that the drawing is a suggestion. The foundation is out by a quarter inch. The wall has a bow in it. The floor slopes toward the back door. You do not get to build the house in the drawing. You build the house that is actually there, and you make it look like it was always meant to be that way.

Measure twice

Carpentry has a short feedback loop and expensive mistakes. Cut the board wrong and you can see it immediately, and the board is gone. That builds a particular kind of care, and a tolerance for doing things in the right order even when the wrong order would be faster today. You do not hang drywall before the rough-in passes inspection.

Finish work

The part I liked most was finish carpentry, which is almost entirely about the last two percent. Nobody notices a good reveal. Everybody notices a bad one.

I still miss it on the days the code will not behave. There is something clarifying about a problem you can solve with a sharp blade and a steady hand.

The Lark

One of my favourite projects was not a house at all. I rebuilt an old Lark camper, which is the kind of job where you open one panel and find three more things that need doing. Everything in a camper is small, nothing is square, and every repair has to survive being towed down the road at sixty.

It scratched the same itch as finish work. Lots of small decisions, all of them visible, none of them hideable behind trim.
