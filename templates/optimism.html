Optimism is a method, not a mood

Five people who changed how I think. This is not a reading list so much as a thank-you note.

Not the naive kind. The kind that assumes a situation can usually be made better, and then goes and tests that. Everybody below taught me some part of how to do that, and the last one turned it into a method.

Alan Watts

The Book: On the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are landed on me hard and has not really left. He is the one who got me to stop taking my own running commentary quite so seriously.

alanwatts.org

Brené Brown

Her work on vulnerability and courage is the reason I am more willing to say "I do not know" in a room full of people who are paying me. The TED talk is the usual entry point and it holds up.

brenebrown.com

Joe Hudson

Joe works with founders and executives, and his whole argument is that becoming a better person and becoming a better leader are the same project. That reframe cost me a few comfortable assumptions. If you only look at one person on this page, make it this one.

joehudson.com

John Wineland

John's work is about the habits we fall into with the people closest to us. Specific, practical, and occasionally uncomfortable in the way useful things tend to be.

johnwineland.com

W. Edwards Deming

Deming is the odd one out. A statistician who spent his career on manufacturing quality, and it took me a while to notice his ideas are at least as useful pointed inward.

Improve constantly, in small boring increments, forever. Drive out fear, including the version you run on yourself, because nobody reports a problem early in a shop where problems get punished, and that is as true inside one head as it is across a team. And the hard one: a lot of what looks like a character flaw is a system problem. Change what you are standing in before you decide what you are.

That is what optimism as a method actually looks like. Not deciding things are fine, but assuming the system can be improved and then going and improving it. The professional side of the same idea is over on Work.

The W. Edwards Deming Institute

Why this page is on a personal website

I have put real work into my own mental wellbeing over the years, with help, and I am not especially private about it. Most of the clearest thinking I have done came after somebody handed me a better way to look at something. This page is me passing that along.
