Optimism is a method, not a mood

Each of these people has had a real effect on how I think and how I work. One of them is a statistician who never meant to end up on a list like this, and he is the reason the rest of it hangs together.

Not the naive kind of optimism. The kind that assumes thoughtful effort and creativity can usually make a situation better, and then goes and tests that assumption. Everybody below taught me some part of how to do that, and the last one turned it into a method.

Joe Hudson

Joe's work with founders and executives is about becoming a better person as the route to becoming a better leader, rather than treating those as two separate projects. He is direct about how much of leadership is emotional skill, and how badly most workplaces handle that. If you only look at one person on this page, make it this one.

joehudson.com

Alan Watts

Watts is the person most responsible for bringing Zen Buddhist ideas to a Western audience in the 1950s. The Book: On the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are landed on me hard and has not really left.

alanwatts.org

Brené Brown

A researcher and an unusually good speaker on vulnerability and courage, particularly in how people communicate at work. Her TED talk is the usual entry point and it holds up.

brenebrown.com

John Wineland

John's work is about relationships and about the habits we fall into when we communicate with the people closest to us. Useful, specific, and occasionally uncomfortable in the way that useful things are.

johnwineland.com

W. Edwards Deming

Deming is the odd one out here. A statistician who spent his career on manufacturing quality, best known for helping rebuild Japanese industry after the war. I came to him through work, and it took me a while to notice his ideas are at least as useful pointed inward.

Improve constantly, in small boring increments, forever. Drive out fear, including the version you run on yourself, because nobody reports a problem early in a shop where problems get punished, and that is as true inside one head as it is across a team. And the hard one: a lot of what looks like a character flaw is a system problem. Change what you are standing in before you decide what you are.

That is what optimism as a method actually looks like. Not deciding things are fine, but assuming the system can be improved and then going and improving it. The studio side of the same idea is over on Work.

The W. Edwards Deming Institute

Why this page is on a personal website

I have put real work into my own mental wellbeing over the years, with help, and I am not especially private about it. Most of the clearest thinking I have done came after somebody handed me a better way to look at something. This page is me passing that along.
