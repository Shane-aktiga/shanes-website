02 · Fly

A life in the air

I have been fascinated by aviation for as long as I can remember, but it was not until 2009 that I finally found my way into the air with a paraglider.

What started as a new adventure quickly became one of my favourite things to do. There is something pretty incredible about launching from a mountain with a wing, a harness and a handful of lines, then using nothing but the air around you to stay up.

It goes back further than that, really. A camp for kids who wanted outdoor skills, then Outward Bound, then a degree in wilderness leadership and business management. Flying is where all of that finally landed in one place.

After all this time I still get excited every time I pull a wing out of its bag.

From pilot to instructor

I started flying in 2009 and became a certified USHPA instructor in 2018, then an advanced instructor in 2021. Teaching gave me an entirely new appreciation for the sport. There is something really rewarding about helping someone build the skills and the confidence to take their first flights.

Through WNC Paragliding I have been able to introduce new pilots to free flight, work with experienced ones, and be part of a community of people who all care about the same odd thing. I have learned a tremendous amount from the people I have flown with over the years.

Most mornings on a training hill end with nobody flying. The air does not turn on, or the wind is wrong, or the cycle never comes. That is the job too. Learning to call it off is a skill, and it is the one that keeps people around long enough to get good.

Smaller wings, bigger smiles

As my flying progressed I started gravitating toward smaller, more dynamic wings. Mini-wings opened up a different side of the sport. They are fast, playful, and a completely different experience from a traditional paraglider.

I also found out how much fun you can have with a wing without ever leaving the ground. Ground handling and kiting became things I enjoy in their own right, especially at the beach.

Then in 2021 Flare introduced the Moustache, a para-kite that brought a new approach to soaring and playing with the wind. That opened up another chapter entirely.

These days I am mostly interested in mini-wings, para-kites and coastal soaring. Smooth ocean wind, working with the terrain, finding out what these wings will do. And yes, apparently I have a thing for collecting increasingly small wings.

Still fascinated by the rest of aviation

My interest in flying does not stop at paragliders. One of the best things free flight has given me is the people, and meeting Al Bowers was a highlight. He spent his career at NASA Armstrong, and his work on bell-shaped spanload distributions, drawn from Prandtl and tested in the Prandtl-D aircraft, is the kind of research that quietly rearranges how you think about wings, birds and adverse yaw.

I spent three days in California filming educational content about his research. He is one of the kindest and most genuinely generous people I have met in aviation, and he explains hard things without ever making you feel small for not already knowing them.

That deepened an interest in the science behind flight that has not gone away since. Sailplanes, electric aviation, and the technologies that might make personal flight more accessible. There is a lot happening, and I enjoy following it.

SkyGear Hub

One of the things I have enjoyed about flying is learning about the equipment behind it. The hard part of buying gear is that there are dozens of manufacturers worldwide and no good way to compare across them for your size, skill level and goals.

I spent about a year building SkyGear Hub to fix that: one place to research and compare wings, harnesses and the rest, with GearMatch for narrowing things down. It launched in 2022 and it is still the project I am most pleased with.

Two things I enjoy, in one place. And like most of my projects, there is always another idea waiting.
